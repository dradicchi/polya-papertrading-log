# Post-Mortem PM-004 — 2026-07-04

**The qualifying event, at last: a +7% rally spread over four to five days
finally tested the tail hedge in the exact scenario it was built for — and the
hedge lost. Across four live adverse events, the realized risk has been the
multi-day grind, never the sudden spike; the hedge program was aimed at the
wrong threat.**

---

## Summary

Between 2026-06-30 and 2026-07-04, BTC rose from approximately 59,050 to 63,240
USD — a **+7.1% move spread over four to five days (~1.5% per day)** that did
not reverse before the window closed. The near-the-money daily short-CALL book
gave back part of its accumulated gains: the two main naked instances returned
about **−0.36 to −0.38 BTC** from their equity peak, the deeper
out-of-the-money instance about **−0.21 BTC** (1 contract per signal, paper).
For the first time since go-live, the **daily drawdown stopping threshold
(5× the historical holdout maximum) was crossed** on several instances.

This is the **first live event of the magnitude the tail hedge was designed
for** (a rally beyond +5%). The three-arm hedge experiment — a naked control
and two protective short-call-spread variants — therefore delivered its first
verdict under the qualifying scenario. The verdict is negative:

- The **naked short leg was resilient**: the byte-identical short leg shared by
  all three arms ended the window at about **−0.037 BTC** cumulative. The pain
  was a path drawdown that largely mean-reverted, not a terminal loss.
- The **hedge was pure cost even here**: the protective leg subtracted about
  **−0.18 BTC** (wider spread, 2 payouts) and **−0.36 BTC** (tighter spread,
  14 payouts). In both cases the premium paid on every trade over the whole
  period dominated the payouts. The naked arm dominated both hedged arms by a
  wide margin (−0.037 vs −0.21 vs −0.40 BTC cumulative).

All instances remained materially positive on a cumulative basis (the two main
naked instances near **+1.0 to +1.1 BTC** since inception). No capital was at
risk — this is paper trading.

## What happened

The strategy is short out-of-the-money CALL. A sustained rally pushes the
near-the-money contracts through their strike, and they settle in-the-money at
the scheduled daily expiration. The damage came from **magnitude delivered
slowly**, not from speed — the same mechanism as PM-003. The per-day velocity
sat above the level at which the book takes damage but, for most of the window,
below the level at which a short call spread becomes useful. The full +7% move
eventually reached even the wider protective strike near the end, which is why
the hedge recorded a handful of payouts — but those payouts never came close to
recovering the premium the hedge had paid on every trade along the way.

## Cross-check against prior events

PM-004 is the **fourth** adverse event on this record, and it confirms a
pattern the first three established:

| Event | Move | Shape | Reversed? |
|---|---|---|---|
| PM-001 | rebound | multi-day | partially, then resolved |
| PM-002 | +4% / 2 days | multi-day | yes — left no mark |
| PM-003 | +9% / 6 days | grind | no — book regenerated |
| PM-004 | +7% / 4–5 days | grind | not within the window |

**Every one of the four was a multi-day move — a grind or a rebound. None was a
sudden intraday spike.** In roughly three months of live operation, the fast
flash-crash-upward scenario that a tail hedge is designed to cut has **not
occurred once**. This is the central observation of PM-004, and it is a
reframing, not just a data point: the hedge program targeted the tail (the
rare, violent spike), while the risk that actually materializes is the body of
the left shoulder (the slow, multi-day grind) — which no cheap hedge covers and
whose premium the tail hedge bleeds continuously. The distinction is the one
Aaron Brown ("Red-Blooded Risk") insists on — audit the whole distribution, not
the average — and it maps onto Bondarenko's (2014) point that short-volatility
carries negative skew, and Spitznagel's / Brown's (2012) point that tail
insurance is paid on every trade and pays back only in the tail.

## What this proves — and does not prove

**Proves:**

- The naked short is resilient even to a sustained +7%: the short leg ended near
  flat and the drawdown mean-reverted. The grind is a **drawdown risk**
  (frequent, material, felt by an allocator, enough to cross a stopping
  threshold) but not a **ruin risk** (the book survives and recovers).
- The tail hedge is expected-value-negative **even in its designed scenario**.
  This is the fourth and strongest live confirmation — no longer inferred from a
  calm market, but measured in the exact event the hedge was meant to cover.

**Does not prove:**

- The genuine fast flash (≥15% in hours) remains **untested** — it has not
  occurred live. That is the only ruin scenario, and the only one where the
  wider spread could be justified — as ruin insurance (a product choice for a
  tail-averse mandate), never on expected value. But it did not materialize,
  while the grind materialized four times.
- Behavior in a sustained bull regime is not established; the live sample is
  bear/sideways.

## Stopping rules — a note on what we did *not* do

The daily drawdown threshold was crossed, and the report flags it. We did **not**
halt trading, and this was deliberate. In all four adverse events to date,
mechanically pausing at the drawdown trigger would have **locked in the loss
near its trough**, immediately before the mean reversion that in fact followed
(PM-001 recovered from a deep drawdown; PM-002 reversed; PM-003 regenerated
within days; PM-004's naked control climbed back from its trough within the same
window). For a mean-reverting short-volatility book, the trough precedes the
recovery, so a naive "drawdown exceeded → stop" rule tends to fire at the worst
possible moment. We therefore treat the stopping thresholds as a **manual-review
alert**, not an automatic trigger. This is a documented design decision, not an
omission.

## Decision

- **No methodology change.** The canonical strategy and the live configuration
  are unchanged. The observed behavior — the naked short absorbing the grind and
  recovering, the hedge bleeding — is expected and acceptable.
- **The tail hedge is not adopted as a return-driven product.** Its live cost,
  measured now under the qualifying event, exceeds its payout. It survives only
  as optional ruin insurance against a fast flash that has not occurred — a
  product decision for a tail-averse mandate, disclosed as such, not an
  expected-value improvement.
- **The primary defense remains position sizing**, not hedging: sizing the book
  so that a grind of this class is a manageable drawdown rather than a threat to
  capital.
